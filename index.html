How decisions actually get made.

This is the decision pattern I keep returning to across the work below. Not because it was planned upfront, but because every shortcut failed. Select any step to inspect the sequence from Glide.

Step 1 · Observe

What problem are we actually solving?

On Glide: travelers were opening more than six tabs just to plan one trip, jumping between airline portals, embassy advisories, and hotel aggregators.

Step 2 · Reduce Uncertainty

What don't we know yet?

Was booking the pain point? Or was it not knowing if the trip would even be permitted? Discovery showed visa anxiety dominated pricing.

Step 3 · Generate Options

What are three real ways this could work?

Option A: Another comparison site. Option B: An itinerary planning assistant. Option C: A unified checkout combining flight + hotel + visa confidence check.

Step 4 · Kill Weak Ideas

Which idea sounds exciting but fails under evidence?

"Another comparison site." It would enter a commoditized market without touching the primary customer anxiety.

REJECTED

This was my favorite idea initially.

Step 5 · Commit

Which trade-off are we willing to accept?

One unified checkout flow, even though integrating multiple disparate APIs meant engineering risk and a longer initial build.

Step 6 · Measure

How will we know this worked?

Trip planning completion time and checkout friction steps, rather than superficial signup counts.

Step 7 · Learn

What would change our mind?

Visa approval confidence mattered more to booking conversion than 5% price discounts ever did.

Case 001 · Travel Architecture

Glide

Version 3 of 3 · Mobile & API Orchestration
The Core Question

Can cross-border travel planning become predictable instead of stressful?

0 of 11 travel platforms audited would tell you whether your visa would actually be approved before you paid for flights and hotels.

Decision Moment 2 of 5

When discovery showed immigration fear dominated trip planning, what would you build first?

Enter full case study ↗
HypothesisA static PDF document checklist would resolve traveler visa anxiety.
EvidenceUser testing showed travelers still asked: "Will I actually get approved?" A static checklist never solved that uncertainty.
DecisionKilled the document checklist. Rebuilt the product around an integrated cart with automated visa eligibility checks.
Trade-offHigher upfront API complexity, but eliminated the root source of booking drop-offs.
Reported Outcome40% fewer booking steps. Shipped on the third complete architecture rewrite.

Case 002 · Workflow Automation

CareerOS

The same decision pattern, a different domain
The Core Question

Can job search coordination be automated without taking away the candidate's agency?

The fastest workflow was not the one candidates trusted. Maximizing raw volume generated noise, not interviews.

Decision Moment 3 of 5

Would you auto-apply on the candidate's behalf, or mandate manual confirmation on every send?

Enter full case study ↗
HypothesisFull auto-apply maximizes application volume and recruiter touches.
Evidence12 automated applications with zero customized relevance yielded 0 replies in 4 days. Candidates felt out of control.
DecisionKilled full automation. Kept automated resume tailoring and company intelligence, but left the final send trigger manual.
Observed MetricObserved telemetry across an initial test cohort: 0 replies on 12 automated submissions vs. 5 replies (31%) across 16 manually verified, tailored applications (5 first-round interviews generated).

Observable Behaviors

What working with me looks like.

"Collaborative" and "data-driven" are empty buzzwords until tied to falsifiable behaviors.

Collaborative

I involve engineering leads before specs become too expensive to change.

Data-driven

I define the primary metric and guardrails before we call any feature successful.

User-centric

I ask what happens if our core assumption is wrong before asking what happens if it's right.

Decisive

I say "we do not know yet" out loud rather than fake a consensus answer.

Scrappy

I ship the smallest surface that can be falsified by real users, not the safest one.

Strategic

I'm willing to say no to three viable features so the team has focus to make an impact.

Hiring Notes

What I'd take away after reviewing this work.

If I were summarizing what I observed across this decision journal to a hiring committee:

01 · Diagnosis

Diagnose before prescribing

Finds the structural bottleneck before jumping to solutions. Distinguishes customer anxiety bottlenecks from superficial UI friction.

02 · Truth-Seeking

Lets evidence change the thesis

Doesn't defend an idea simply because it was the original plan. Changes direction when customer or product evidence invalidates the initial bet.

03 · Clarity

Makes trade-offs explicit

Can explain what was chosen, what was deliberately left out, and why. Prevents consensus paralysis by forcing falsifiable decisions.

04 · Architecture

Thinks across the entire journey

Looks beyond individual isolated features to understand the technical, commercial, and operational systems surrounding them.

05 · Accountability

Connects decisions to outcomes

Uses metrics to determine whether the decision actually worked. Drives releases to completion through API and operational dependencies.
